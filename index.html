What is the most likely diagnosis?

Select one answer to reveal the expert explanation.

The answer is C — posterior mediastinal mass. Key clues to localise it:

  • A well-defined, rounded opacity projects over the left mediastinum, yet the left heart border and aortic contour remain visible — no silhouette sign, so it isn’t in contact with these anterior/middle structures.
  • Hilar vessels can be traced through the opacity (hilum overlay sign), arguing against true hilar enlargement.
  • A sharp, convex interface with the lung — unlike consolidation, there are no air bronchograms or ill-defined margins.
  • Next step: lateral view and cross-sectional imaging. Posterior mediastinal masses are most often neurogenic tumours.

Join us on 22 January for the full walkthrough of this and many more cases in “Hila and Mediastinum: Localise the Abnormality”.

Agenda

A structured evening that moves from normal anatomy to a systematic approach, common abnormalities and clinical traps.
